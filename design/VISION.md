# Mercantile — Vision

> *"Every villager remembers."* — a villager and trade overhaul: the people you
> buy from become people you have a history with.

## The promise

Mercantile takes over Minecraft's villagers and trading. Vanilla villagers are
lever-operated vending machines — nameless, rooted to a workstation, indifferent
to whether you trade fairly or cut them down. With Mercantile installed every
villager has a name, can be picked up and carried home in your hand, and keeps a
running memory of how you've treated them that follows you across the whole
world. Trading stops being a chore of smashing and replacing job blocks: you
re-roll offers, buy in bulk, pin the trade you're waiting on, and watch prices
move with your standing. If you want villages that feel inhabited instead of
farmed, Mercantile is for you.

## The arc of a world

In your first hours you notice the little things: villagers have names floating
above them, and picking one up to carry it somewhere safer is a sneak-click away.
You trade, and the game quietly starts keeping score — a small badge in the
corner tells you where you stand the moment a villager is near.

By the mid game you're running a village, not visiting one. You re-roll a
villager onto the trade you actually want, put idle villagers to work with a
job-site block, and pin the enchanted book you're hunting so you're pinged the
moment it's back in stock. Your standing has climbed to Trusted, and trades you
couldn't see before have opened up. You fuel a Sentry Pylon and let iron golems
answer the next raid.

The late game is a village that knows you. At Honored prices bottom out, every
exclusive trade is on the table — including pre-enchanted gear — and villagers
start walking over to hand you thank-you gifts unprompted. Cross a villager and
you feel the other edge of the same system: keepsakes drop where the named dead
fall, neighbors stop to mourn, and a village that has watched you kill marks up
its prices to your face until the fear fades.

## The features, as you'll play them

### Villagers you can carry
- **What it is** — every villager and wandering trader is named on sight, and any
  of them can be picked up and carried as a head item with everything about them
  intact: their job, level, trades, and the standing and gossip they hold.
- **How you use it** — sneak-right-click a villager with an empty hand to pick it
  up (a small experience cost, 5 by default); right-click a block to set it back
  down facing you. Hold an emerald and sneak-right-click instead to make one
  follow you — it trails about 6 blocks back. Sneak-click with an emerald again to
  send it home to its own bed.
- **What changes for you** — moving your trading hall becomes carrying a stack of
  heads, not herding villagers through boats and minecarts. Pickup is refused
  mid-raid, mid-trade, or while a villager already follows someone, so nobody
  steals your traders. Followers auto-release if you get more than 32 blocks
  ahead, and up to 3 can trail you at once.

### Putting villagers to work
- **What it is** — direct job assignment instead of workstation roulette, plus a
  way to rescue the useless.
- **How you use it** — sneak-right-click an unemployed villager while holding a
  job-site block (a lectern, a composter, any of them) and it walks to the
  nearest free workstation of that type and takes that profession for a 1-emerald
  fee. At Trusted standing you can hand a nitwit a golden apple and pay 16
  emeralds to turn it into an ordinary, employable villager.
- **What changes for you** — you decide who does which job, rather than breaking
  and replacing job blocks until the game hands you the profession you wanted.
  Nitwits stop being dead weight you cull.

### Trading, reworked
- **What it is** — the trading screen becomes something you can plan around:
  re-rolled offers, bulk purchases, restock timers, pinned trades, and an honest
  price breakdown.
- **How you use it** — pay 6 emeralds to re-roll a villager's unlocked trades from
  its profession pool; shift-click a trade output to buy in bulk (up to 64 at a
  locked price); click the pin beside any trade to be told on your action bar the
  moment that villager restocks it, even from up to 128 blocks away. Hover any
  price to see exactly what's moving it.
- **What changes for you** — no more smashing lecterns for a Mending book. The
  price breakdown names every discount and markup — demand, your standing, the
  villager's mood, gossip, market day, fear — so a price is never a mystery. Every
  seventh day the world holds a Market Day: a global discount from dawn to dusk,
  announced with a bell, with an extra restock for every villager.

### Reputation — every villager remembers
- **What it is** — a single standing with villagerkind, from −200 to +1500 across
  five tiers, that survives death and travels the world with you.
- **How you use it** — trade fairly, cure zombie villagers, defend a village from a
  raid, or toss a villager a profession item it wants, and your standing climbs;
  attack or kill villagers and it falls. A badge in the corner shows your tier
  whenever a villager is within 32 blocks; hold the peek key (V by default) for
  the full breakdown — exact score, progress to the next tier, today's earnings,
  and which exclusive trades you've unlocked.
- **What changes for you** — standing is both a discount curve and a gate. Liked
  shaves 5% off; Trusted gives 10% and opens profession-exclusive trades; Honored
  gives 15%, every exclusive trade including enchanted gear, and unprompted
  gratitude gifts. Slip to Distrusted and prices climb; hit Reviled and villagers
  refuse you outright — though a run of gifts, or simply time, pulls a negative
  score back toward neutral.

### When a villager dies
- **What it is** — death carries weight and memory.
- **How you use it** — nothing to trigger; it happens when a named villager dies
  near others. It drops a keepsake naming who they were and how they fell, nearby
  villagers stop to mourn, and a village that watches you kill several of its own
  in a short window (3 within 10 minutes) turns afraid of you.
- **What changes for you** — fear is a markup only you pay, only in that village
  (25% by default), fading over a few days and shown as its own line in the price
  breakdown, stacked on top of the reputation hit. Your reputation stops being
  just a number — in a village you've wronged, it's local and physical.

### Delivery contracts
- **What it is** — villagers occasionally ask something of *you*.
- **How you use it** — when a villager floats a request bubble, sneak and use paper
  on it to sign a contract for a specific haul (say, 32 wheat) by a deadline (2
  days by default). While you hold the contract the villager who wants it glows
  through walls. Right-click the payee with the goods anywhere in your bag to
  deliver.
- **What changes for you** — a reason to go gather that pays in emeralds and a
  standing bonus, with the villager marked so you never lose track of who is
  waiting. Miss the deadline and the contract simply crumbles — no penalty.

### The Sentry Pylon
- **What it is** — the mod's one new block: an iron-fueled tower that defends your
  village with temporary iron golems.
- **How you use it** — craft it from a carved pumpkin, iron blocks, a bell, and
  stone bricks; right-click iron blocks into it to fuel it (up to 8 stored). When
  a hostile it can *see* comes within 32 blocks it spends an iron block to summon
  a golem (up to 3 at once) and rings the nearest bell to call you to the fight.
- **What changes for you** — hands-off village defense that even handles creepers
  without cratering your town. Golems leash to the pylon, drop nothing, and fade
  30 seconds after the last threat is gone. Redstone switches the pylon off; a
  comparator reads its fuel.

## Staying in control

Every feature above is individually switchable — turn off pickup, reputation, the
Sentry Pylon, market day, mourning, or any single one, and the rest carry on. The
numbers are yours to tune: the experience cost of a pickup, every reputation gain
and loss, the market-day interval, the fear markup and how long it lasts, the
healing boost potions give villagers, how fast fed babies grow up.

The corner badge is opt-out on its own, and a volume slider mutes villager sounds
without touching any other audio. A short command set stays out of your way:
`/mercantile reputation` tells any player their own standing and `/mercantile
pins` manages your pinned trades, while server operators also get
`/mercantile reputation set` and `add`, per-player standing lookups, and
`/mercantile reload` to apply changed settings without a restart. Leave everything
at its defaults and Mercantile assumes a fair single- or multiplayer game where
villages remember kindness and cruelty alike — and nothing about vanilla you
didn't ask it to change is changed.

## Better together

- **With Tribulation** — the Sentry Pylon reads the local danger and scales its
  golems and reach to match harder raids, so your defenses keep pace with the
  threat. Never required.
- **With Meridian** — high-standing librarians become an on-ramp into its
  enchanting: they sell salvage tomes, shelf materials, and rolled enchanted books
  whose pools widen as your reputation grows. Never required.
- **With Prosperity** — the surplus you haul out of the world is exactly what
  Mercantile's economy converts into what you're missing. Never required.
- **With a tooltip mod (Jade or WTHIT) or a recipe viewer (EMI, REI, or JEI)** —
  villager tooltips gain breeding, mood, and status lines, and every trade in the
  game becomes a searchable, filterable index. Never required.

With no siblings installed nothing is missing — every headline feature above is
Mercantile's alone and stands on its own.

## What Mercantile will never do

- **Never turn standing into loot or cheap enchants.** Reputation is village
  standing: it buys discounts and village-only trades, never better dungeon loot
  and never cheaper enchanting. Those belong to other systems, and mixing them
  cheapens both.
- **Never add new professions or a new currency.** Villagers keep their vanilla
  jobs and emeralds stay the coin, so a trading hall you know still reads the way
  you expect.
- **Never scale the raids themselves.** Mercantile builds the *defense* — the
  pylon, the golems, the bell. How hard a raid hits is a different mod's job.
- **Never add a dimension, and never require a companion mod.** No new worlds to
  visit, no second mod you must install. Remove any sibling and you lose only that
  sibling's flavor.
- **Never clutter your screen.** One small badge you can switch off is the whole
  footprint — no always-on panels, no skill trees.

A feature request that collides with these lines is reshaped or declined — or this
page is amended first, deliberately.
